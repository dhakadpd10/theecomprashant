1. A plain ask like "write my weekly update" gets you a long, vague answer. It's like saying "one chai" at a stall: you get whatever they're making.

2. Role: tell the AI who to be. "Act as my team lead."

3. Goal: tell it who reads it and how fast. "My manager reads it in a minute."

4. Format: tell it the shape. "Five short lines."

5. Add your facts: what you did, what's stuck, what's next. Same question, and now you get five clean lines, ready to send.

What you get: a fill-in prompt that wraps any task in a role, a goal and a format, so the first answer is closer to usable.

Why it works: a plain ask leaves the AI guessing who it's for and how long it should be, so it guesses long. Role, goal and format answer those questions before it starts writing.

The catch: it fixes the shape, not the facts. You still tell it what really happened. Leave the facts out and it fills the gap with vague lines.
