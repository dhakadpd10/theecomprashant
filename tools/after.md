# 3 After-Delivery Messages

You are writing the three messages a buyer should get after their order is delivered. The goal is fewer returns, fewer angry reviews, more honest reviews with photos, and a buyer who feels looked after. You are not writing sales messages.

**Step 1: Get the facts first.** Read what I paste at the bottom. Check that you have each item below. If something is missing, ask me for it in one short list before you write anything. If I tell you to go ahead without it, write [CHECK: what's missing] in that spot. Never make up a policy, time limit, phone number, link, discount or product detail.

1. Brand name and the name of the person or team signing off.
2. The product: what it is, what it's made of, and who it's for.
3. How to use it, set it up or care for it (washing, storage, first use, things that damage it).
4. Fit or size details, if it has any. Also the most common problems buyers report.
5. Exchange policy: how many days, how to ask, who pays for shipping, and whether it applies to COD orders.
6. Return or refund policy, if it differs from the exchange policy.
7. Support contact: WhatsApp number, email, and hours.
8. Review link (your own site, Google, or wherever you collect reviews).
9. Any reward for leaving a review, and its exact terms. If there is no reward, say so.
10. Channels: WhatsApp, email, or both. If WhatsApp, say whether it's the free WhatsApp Business App or a paid tool (Interakt, Wati, AiSensy, Gallabox, etc.).
11. Language: English, Hindi, Hinglish, or another one. Also tone: warm, playful, or premium.
12. Where these orders come from. Your own site only, or marketplaces too?

**Step 2: Write the three messages.** Count every day from the **delivery date**, not the order date.

**Day 0, delivery day: "Here's how to get the most out of it."**
- Thank them in one line.
- Give 2 or 3 how-to-use or care tips, taken only from what I told you.
- Tell them who to message if something is wrong.
- Don't sell anything, don't offer discounts and don't ask for a review.

**Day 3: "Is it right?"**
- Ask one simple question about fit, size or quality.
- Make problems easy to raise. Ask them to reply with a photo, or a word like EXCHANGE or HELP.
- Add one clear exchange line with the real policy: how many days are left and what they need to do. Use [CHECK: ...] if I haven't confirmed it.
- Write it so an unhappy buyer replies to you before they write a 1-star review or start a return.

**Day 10: "Would you share a photo?"**
- Send this only to buyers who did not report a problem on Day 3.
- Ask for an honest review and a photo of the product in use. Keep the ask to one line, and include the review link.
- If there is a reward, it must be for any honest review, never only for good ones. State the exact terms.
- If the orders came from Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho or another marketplace, don't write a Day 10 message for them. Tell me to check that marketplace's rules on contacting buyers and asking for reviews, because they usually restrict both.

**For each message, give me:**
- **WhatsApp version:** under 60 words, one ask, at most 1 emoji. If I use a paid WhatsApp tool, also give a template version with {{1}}, {{2}}-style placeholders and a list of what each placeholder means. Paid tools usually need Meta to approve templates before you can send them.
- **Email version:** subject line (under 45 characters), preview text, then a body under 120 words.
- **Category guess:** for WhatsApp templates, say whether Meta will likely treat this as "utility" or "marketing", and why. Mark it [CHECK: Meta decides the final category at approval and may change it]. A review request or anything with an offer is often treated as marketing.
- **Skip if:** who should not get this message.

**Step 3: Skip rules.** List who gets none of these messages:
- cancelled orders
- RTO (returned to origin) orders
- buyers with a return or complaint open
- buyers who replied STOP
- buyers who never agreed to receive WhatsApp messages from you
