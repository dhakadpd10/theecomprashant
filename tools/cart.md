# Cart Comeback Sequence

You load the messages into your own WhatsApp tool (Interakt, Wati, AiSensy, a Shopify app or similar), and only for shoppers who opted in to WhatsApp messages from your brand.

---

You are writing a 3-message abandoned-cart WhatsApp sequence for one product. The goal is to win back shoppers who added it to their cart and left, without spamming them or lying to them. You write copy only. You do not send anything, and you never claim the sequence will recover any number of carts.

**Ground rules (follow these the whole way):**
- Use only facts I give you. Never invent prices, discounts, reviews, ratings, customer counts, delivery times, stock levels, guarantees or return terms. If a message needs a fact I didn't give, write [CHECK: what I need to confirm] in its place.
- No fake urgency. No made-up countdowns, "only 2 left" or "price going up" unless I told you it is true.
- No discount unless I gave you one. If I didn't, the sequence works without one.
- Every message must say how to stop messages (for example: "Reply STOP to stop these messages").
- Plain words, short lines, one link per message, one ask per message. Write it so it reads well on a phone.

**Step 1: Check opt-in first.** Ask me: "Did these shoppers agree to get WhatsApp messages from your brand (for example a ticked box at checkout or login)?"
- If the answer is no or I'm not sure, stop. Don't write the sequence. Instead, write a short, clear opt-in line I can add at checkout, plus one sentence saying why I need it. Then end there.
- If the answer is yes, go on. Remind me in one line that abandoned-cart messages usually have to be sent as approved WhatsApp templates through my provider, and that I should [CHECK: template category and approval rules with my WhatsApp provider].

**Step 2: Read my inputs.** Read everything I pasted at the bottom. If something important is missing (price, shipping cost, COD or not, returns, real proof I can use), list what's missing and ask me once. If I say "skip", carry on and mark the gaps as [CHECK: ...]. Don't guess.

**Step 3: Write two versions of each message.** Write Version A and Version B for each of these three messages. Use placeholders like {{first_name}}, {{product_name}} and {{cart_link}} so it works in any WhatsApp tool. Keep each message under 60 words.

1. **Message 1: The doubt answer.** Answer the most likely reason they left, using the objections I listed (price, size, COD, delivery, trust, quality). No pressure. Sound like a helpful shop owner, not a robot.
2. **Message 2: The proof.** Show one real reason to trust the product: a review I pasted (word for word, with first name or initial only if I gave it), a real number I gave you, or a plain fact about the product. If I gave you no proof, use a product fact and add [CHECK: add a real review here].
3. **Message 3: The fair last nudge.** Say clearly that this is the last message about this cart. Remind them what they picked and make it easy to buy or to say no. Only mention a deadline or offer if I gave you a real one.

Suggest a send time for each one as a starting point to test, not a rule. Default: Message 1 about 1 hour after they leave, Message 2 about 24 hours after, Message 3 about 48 to 72 hours after. No messages between 9pm and 9am in the shopper's time zone (IST unless I say otherwise).

**Step 4: Five shoppers who left a cart vote.** Read both versions of each message five times, each time as one of these shoppers. Each shopper votes alone and never sees the others' votes.

1. **COD doubter:** wants to pay on delivery and isn't sure the brand is real. Does this make me trust you?
2. **Price comparer:** saw something similar on Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho for less. Why should I buy here?
3. **Payday waiter:** liked it and is waiting for salary day. Does this push me or remember me kindly?
4. **Fit or quality worrier:** scared it won't fit, suit them or match the photos. Does this answer my real worry?
5. **Spam hater:** gets ten brand messages a day and blocks fast. Is this worth reading, or am I blocking you?

For each shopper and each message, write:
- **Picks:** A or B.
- **Would:** reply, tap the link, ignore, or block.
- **Because:** one sentence, in their own voice.

Then show a table: rows are the three messages, columns are the five shoppers, cells show their pick and what they'd do. Add a line saying which version wins each message, and flag any message where two or more shoppers would block.

Say clearly that these are simulated shoppers, not real customer data, and that only real sends will show what works.

**Step 5: The final sequence.** Take the winning version of each message and improve it using what the shoppers said, especially the spam hater. For each message give:
- The final copy, ready to paste.
- When to send it.
- What it's for (one line).

- places the order (on any channel I can track),
- replies STOP or asks not to be messaged,
- replies with a question or complaint (a human should answer, not the sequence),
- empties the cart.

After Message 3, no more messages about this cart. A shopper should not enter this sequence again for [CHECK: how many days I want, suggest 30] after finishing it.
