You are helping me, a D2C store owner, run one Click-to-WhatsApp ad on Meta (Facebook or Instagram). When someone taps the ad, a WhatsApp chat with my business opens. You will write the ad, my first reply, and 3 follow-up messages for people who don't buy straight away. You will also make a one-page checklist of which messages are likely free and which may be charged.

You draft. I send. You never send messages, and you never promise that any tool will send them on a schedule.

**Ground rules (follow these in every step):**
- Never invent facts about my product, price, offer, stock, delivery times, reviews, ratings, certifications or guarantees. If you need something I haven't given you, write [CHECK: what I need to add] and keep going.
- Never state WhatsApp or Meta pricing, policy or limits as certain. Write what you understand, then mark it [CHECK: confirm on Meta's official page]. WhatsApp pricing changed on 1 October 2026. If you don't know exactly what changed, say so plainly. Don't guess.
- No fake urgency. No made-up countdowns, fake "only 3 left", or invented discounts. Only use urgency I give you (for example, a real sale end date or a real courier cutoff).
- No claims Meta ads policy is likely to reject. That includes health or body claims, "before and after" promises, and lines that guess at the reader's personal traits ("Are you overweight?", "Struggling with debt?"). If my brief asks for one, flag it and give a safer version.
- Every follow-up must be easy to refuse. Give one clear way to stop (for example, "Reply STOP and I won't message again") and respect it.
- Plain words, short sentences, my brand's voice. Use rupees (₹). Hindi or Hinglish only if I ask for it.

**Step 2: The ad.** Write 3 versions, each with a different angle:
- **A. The problem:** the buyer's everyday frustration that the product fixes.
- **B. The question:** lead with the question buyers ask most, and answer it in the chat.
- **C. The proof:** only if I gave you real reviews, ratings or numbers. If I didn't, write [CHECK: add a real review or skip this version]. Don't make one up.

For each version, give:
- **Primary text:** 2 to 4 short lines. Say why they should tap to chat, not just "Buy now".
- **Headline:** short and specific.
- **Pre-filled message:** the text that appears in the customer's WhatsApp box when the chat opens, written so they just hit send (for example, "Hi, I want to know my size for the cotton kurta"). Keep it under one line.
- **Why tap:** one sentence on why chatting beats visiting the website for this product.

**Step 3: The first reply (send within 5 minutes if you can).** Write the reply I send when someone messages from the ad. It must:
- Answer what the pre-filled message asked, in 3 to 5 short lines.
- Ask one easy question that moves toward the order (size, colour, pin code for delivery time).
- Give price, payment options and delivery time only from my brief, otherwise [CHECK: ...].
- Not dump a catalogue or 5 links.

Also write 3 short quick replies for the likely next questions: price or discount, COD, and returns. Use only facts from my brief.

**Step 4: The 3 follow-ups.** These are for people who chatted and didn't order. Stop the sequence for anyone who orders, says no, or says stop. Lay out the timing as a table: **Message | Send at | Window it falls in | Message type needed**. Count every time from the customer's first message.

- **Follow-up 1: about 3 to 6 hours in.** Pick up their exact question or hesitation from the chat. Leave a slot like [THEIR QUESTION] for me to fill. One helpful line, one easy next step.
- **Follow-up 2: about 24 to 30 hours in.** Handle the most common objection for this product (price, size, trust in COD, delivery time). Use only real facts. Offer one useful thing: a size answer, a photo of the product in use [CHECK: do I have one?], or a real offer.
- **Follow-up 3: about 60 to 66 hours in.** The last message. Polite, no pressure. Make it easy to order or easy to stop. Leave a buffer before hour 72 so it doesn't land outside the window.

- Whether I can send it as a normal free-form message, or whether it must be a pre-approved **template message**.
