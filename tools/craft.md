# The Readable-Text Poster Prompt

Paste everything below the line into Claude, then fill in the brackets in "My poster details." Claude checks your words and price, then gives you a ready prompt to paste into Canva's Dream Lab or any image AI. It ends with a 4-point check to run before you print or post. Image AIs still misspell words sometimes, so never skip the check.

---

You are helping me make a poster for my store with an image AI (Canva Dream Lab or any other). Image AIs often misspell words, change prices or add text nobody asked for. Your job is to write a prompt that gives the words the best chance of coming out right, and then help me catch any mistakes before the poster goes out.

**Rules for you:**
- Use my words exactly as I typed them inside quotes. Do not reword, shorten or "improve" my headline, price or offer unless I ask.
- Never invent a price, discount, offer date, review, award, certification, "bestseller" badge or guarantee. If something is missing or unclear, write [CHECK: what I need to confirm] and ask me.
- Example values in this prompt are marked "(example)." They are not my real prices.
- Plain words. No hype.

**Step 1: Read my details.** Read "My poster details" at the bottom. If any field is empty, ask me for it in one short list before you write anything. Don't guess.

**Step 2: Check my text before it goes into the prompt.** Show me a short report:
- **Spelling:** any word that looks misspelt, including brand and product names. Ask me to confirm, don't silently fix.
- **Price format:** is every price written the same way (for example "₹499" (example), not "Rs 499" in one place and "499/-" in another)? Is the ₹ symbol in place? If I show an old price and a new price, remind me the old price must be one I really charged.
- **Too many words:** image AIs get worse as text gets longer. Flag any line over 6 words and the poster if it has more than 4 text lines in total. Suggest a shorter version, and keep mine as an option too.
- **Claims that need proof:** words like "best," "No. 1," "100% natural," "free delivery," "lowest price" or "limited stock." Mark each one [CHECK: can you prove this?].
- **Numbers and dates:** offer end date, discount %, phone number, website. List each one so I can confirm it.

Wait for me to confirm or correct before Step 3.

**Step 3: Write the image prompt.** Give me three versions, each in its own copy box:

1. **Full prompt (text in the image).** Include:
   - The size and shape I asked for (for example, square 1080 x 1080 for an Instagram post, 1080 x 1920 for a story or reel cover, A4 portrait for print).
   - Who it's for and the mood, in one line.
   - Each text line in double quotes, word for word, with its position (top, centre, bottom, left, right), its relative size (biggest, medium, small) and its colour.
   - "Use only the text in quotes. Add no other words, letters, logos, badges or numbers."
   - "Large, bold, simple sans-serif letters. High contrast with the background. Plain area behind every line of text."
   - My colours (hex codes if I gave them) and what each colour is used for.
   - What the product looks like, from my description only. If I didn't describe it, write [CHECK: describe the product or upload a photo].
   - Anything I said must NOT appear.
2. **Short prompt.** The same poster in under 60 words, for tools that cut long prompts.
3. **No-text background prompt.** The same scene and colours with "no text, no letters, no numbers," and empty space left where each line will go. Tell me to add the words myself with Canva's normal text tool. This is the safest way to get the spelling 100% right.

Under the three boxes, add one line: "If the AI gets a word wrong twice, stop retrying. Use the no-text background and type the words yourself."

**Step 4: The 4-point check before you print or post.** Give me this checklist, filled in with my real text, so I can tick it against the finished image:

1. **Spelling, letter by letter.** Compare every word on the poster with my quoted text. Read it backwards, word by word, so your eyes don't autocorrect. Brand name, product name and any Hindi or regional words get checked twice.
2. **Price.** The number, the ₹ symbol, and no extra or missing digits ("₹499" (example) has not become "₹4999" or "₹49"). It matches the price on my store or at my counter today. Any old price shown is one I really charged.
3. **Every other number.** Discount %, offer end date, phone number, website and pin code all match what I confirmed in Step 2.
4. **Nothing extra.** No made-up words, gibberish letters, fake logos, fake badges ("certified," "award winner") or text I never asked for. Then shrink the image to phone size and check every line is still readable. For print, stand 2 metres back and check the headline.

If any point fails, tell me which version from Step 3 to use next.

**My poster details:**
- Who it's for (one line): [for example, college students near my shop, or mothers buying school bags]
- What the poster is for: [sale / new launch / festive offer / menu / price list / other]
- Product or shop, described in words: [what it looks like, colour, packaging, or "I'll upload a photo"]
- Headline, exactly: "[TYPE HEADLINE HERE]"
- Price, exactly: "[for example ₹499 (example). Type your real price]"
- Old price, if any, exactly: "[leave blank if none]"
- Offer line, exactly: "[for example Valid till 15 Nov (example). Leave blank if none]"
- Other text lines, exactly (keep to 2 or fewer): "[shop name / website / phone]"
- Where each line goes: [for example headline top centre, price bottom right, shop name bottom left]
- Colours: [for example navy #1B2A4A and yellow #F5C518 (example), or "my brand colours are …"]
- Size: [Instagram post 1080 x 1080 / story 1080 x 1920 / A4 print / WhatsApp status / other]
- Style: [clean and simple / festive / bold sale / premium]
- Must NOT appear: [for example no people, no other brands, no extra text]
