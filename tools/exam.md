# Date-Sheet to Study Timetable Prompt

Paste everything below the line into Claude, then paste your exam date sheet and syllabus where it says (or attach clear photos of them) and fill in the 3 blanks at the bottom. It works for college semesters, school boards, university exams and entrance tests.

---

You are building a day-by-day study timetable from my exam date sheet and syllabus. It has to be realistic and honest, and it should be easy to follow on a phone.

**Step 1: Read my date sheet.** Read the date sheet I pasted or attached. List every paper in a table with these columns: Date, Day, Time, Subject, Paper or subject code.
- Copy dates and times exactly as they appear. Do not guess or fill gaps.
- If anything is blurry, cut off or unclear, write [CHECK: what is unclear] in that cell.
- Ask me for today's date and the date I want to start. Do not assume today's date.
- Show me this table and wait for me to confirm it before you plan anything. A wrong exam date ruins the whole timetable.

**Step 2: Read my syllabus.** For each subject, list the units or chapters exactly as my syllabus names them.
- Do not add chapters that are not in what I gave you. Do not remove any either.
- If I did not give a syllabus for a subject, write [CHECK: chapters for SUBJECT] and plan that subject in equal blocks until I fill it in.
- If the syllabus shows marks or weightage, note it. If it doesn't, do not invent it. Write [CHECK: marks weightage] instead.

**Step 3: Build the timetable with these rules.**
1. **Hours:** never plan more than my daily study hours. Split each day into slots of 45 to 90 minutes with a short break between them.
2. **Weak subjects first:** start my 2 weak subjects earliest in the plan, give them more total time, and put them in the first slot of the day, when I am freshest.
3. **Revision before each paper:** keep at least 1 full revision day just before each exam, for that subject only. If two papers have no gap between them, say so clearly. Then plan a shorter revision block on the evening before each one.
4. **1 buffer day a week:** plan no new topics on it. It is only for catching up on anything I missed. If I am on track, I use it for past papers or extra revision.
5. **Off days stay off:** plan nothing on my off days, and never move study into them without asking me.
6. **Exam days:** on the day of a paper, plan only light revision for the next paper, or nothing, never new topics.
7. **Sleep:** no late nights or all-nighters in the plan. Never suggest studying more than the hours I gave you.

**Step 4: Do the time check before showing the plan.** Add up the study hours I have and compare them with what the syllabus needs.
- If there is enough time, say so in one line.
- If there is not enough time, say it plainly, with the numbers. Then suggest what to cover first and what to cover lightly. Base this only on the weightage I gave or on my weak subjects. Never pretend everything will fit.

**Step 5: Show the timetable.** Make one table with these columns:
Date | Day | Type (Study, Revision, Buffer, Off, Exam) | Slot 1 | Slot 2 | Slot 3 (add more slots if my hours need them) | Total hours

In each study slot, write the subject and the exact chapter or unit, for example "Maths: Unit 3, Integration". Make every row fit on one phone screen.

Under the table, add:
- **Week view:** one line per week saying what gets finished that week.
- **Mark it done:** a short note telling me to tick each slot as I finish it, so the Sunday re-plan is accurate.
- **Double-check:** remind me to match the dates against my college or board's official notice, because date sheets do get revised.

**Step 6: End with the Sunday re-plan line.** Give me this line, ready to copy, and tell me to paste it back into this same chat every Sunday or whenever I fall behind:

`Sunday re-plan: Today is [DATE]. Done: [chapters I finished]. Not done: [chapters I missed]. Hours I can study per day from now: [HOURS]. Re-plan from tomorrow with the same rules and the same exam dates.`

When I paste that line back:
- Keep every exam date fixed.
- Move the missed chapters into buffer days first, then spread the rest across the study days left.
- Keep the revision days before each paper.
- Run the time check again and tell me honestly if I am still on track.
- Show only the updated days, not the whole plan again.

Keep the language simple. No motivational speeches. If I seem stressed, say one kind, practical line and keep going.
