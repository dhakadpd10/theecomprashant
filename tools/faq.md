# Size Guide and FAQ Writer

Paste everything below the line into Claude or ChatGPT. Then fill in the boxes at the bottom with your size chart, your policies, and your support chats, return reasons and reviews. Before you paste, delete customer names, phone numbers, order IDs and addresses. The output is text you copy into your Shopify product page (description or FAQ tab) yourself. It does not touch your store.

---

You are writing a size guide and an FAQ for one product, or one product type, on my online store. You work only from what I paste below. Your job is to turn what real buyers said into words that stop the next buyer from ordering the wrong size, or sending us the same question again.

**Rules for every step:**
- Never invent facts. That includes measurements, fabric, delivery times, prices, return windows, guarantees and offers. If something is needed and I didn't give it to you, write [CHECK: what I need to confirm].
- Never write fake reviews or testimonials. You may quote short phrases from buyers, but never include a name, phone number, order ID, address or anything else that identifies a person. If you see personal details, leave them out and tell me in the notes at the end.
- Only promise what my policy says. Don't write "free returns", "easy exchange" or "delivered in 3 days" unless my policy says exactly that.
- Count, don't guess. If you say "most buyers", show the count, for example "9 of 14 fit comments".
- If the data is too thin, say so. For example, fewer than about 10 comments about fit isn't enough to call a pattern. Tell me plainly what to collect instead of filling the gap with guesses.
- Be honest about fit, even if it's bad news. If the product runs small, the guide says so. That costs less than a return.

**Step 1: Check what I gave you.**
List what you received:
- How many support chats, return reasons and reviews.
- Whether I gave you a size chart with real measurements.
- Which policies I gave you: returns, exchange, shipping time, COD, payment.

If the size chart or the policies are missing, tell me before you continue. Then carry on, and mark every gap with [CHECK: ...].

**Step 2: Sort the sizing evidence.**
Go through every chat, return reason and review that mentions fit. Make a table with these columns:
- Size bought
- What the buyer said about themselves (height, weight, usual size, or the brand they compared to), only if they said it
- What happened (too tight, too loose, too short, too long, fit well)
- Where on the body (chest, waist, hips, length, sleeves, toes, width, and so on)
- Source (chat, return or review)

Below the table, write the patterns. Only call something a pattern if at least 3 different buyers said it. List anything said once or twice separately as "mentioned once" or "mentioned twice".

**Step 3: Sort the questions.**
Group every question buyers asked by topic. Topics might include sizing, fabric and washing, colour compared with the photo, delivery time, COD, returns and exchange, gifting, and stock. Count how often each one comes up.

Then:
- Pick the 8 most-asked questions.
- Separately, list any question that comes up often but that my policies don't answer.

**Step 4: Write the size guide in buyers' words.**
Include these parts, in this order:
1. **One-line fit verdict.** For example, "Fits true to size" or "Runs small in the chest. If you're between sizes, size up." Only write a verdict if Step 2 supports it. Otherwise write [CHECK: not enough fit data yet].
2. **"If you're..." lines (3 to 6 of them).** For example: "If you're 5'4" and usually wear M, most buyers your size kept the M." Every line must trace back to at least one real buyer in Step 2. Use the units buyers used (feet and inches, kg, UK or Indian shoe sizes). Don't convert to measurements I didn't give you.
3. **Size chart.** Copy my measurements exactly, without changing them. If I didn't give a chart, write [CHECK: add chest, waist and length in cm and inches for each size], adapted to the product.
4. **How to measure.** Three short steps, only for the measurements my chart actually uses.
5. **Still unsure line.** Point buyers to my exchange policy, worded exactly as my policy says, and to my contact: [CHECK: your WhatsApp number or support email].

Keep it under 150 words, not counting the table. If the product has no sizes (a bag or a candle, for example), write a short "dimensions and what fits inside" guide instead, using the same rules.

**Step 5: Write the 8 FAQs.**
- Write each question the way a buyer would type it, for example "COD available?" or "Will it shrink after wash?"
- Answer in at most 2 short sentences, taken only from my policies and product details.
- If my policies don't answer it, the answer is [CHECK: ...]. Don't fill the gap.
- Keep the source notes out of the FAQ itself. Put them in Step 7.

**Step 6: Ready to paste.**
Give me two clean blocks I can copy straight into Shopify:
- The size guide.
- The FAQ.

Formatting:
- Use simple headings, short lines and bullet points that paste cleanly into the Shopify description editor or a collapsible FAQ tab.
- No emojis, no hype words, no exclamation marks.
- Match the tone of my current product description if I gave you one. Otherwise use plain, friendly language.

**Step 7: Notes for me (not for the page).**
- **[CHECK] list:** every [CHECK] item in one list, so I can fill them in before publishing.
- **FAQ sources:** for each of the 8 questions, how many times it was asked and which policy line the answer came from.
- **Problems outside the page:** issues the page can't fix. For example: "6 returns say the colour is darker than the photo. Reshoot in daylight." Or: "4 buyers say the sleeves are short. Check with your tailor or supplier."
- **What to collect next:** what to record for a better guide next time. For example, ask buyers who exchange for their height and usual size.
- **Personal details removed:** what you left out, if anything.
