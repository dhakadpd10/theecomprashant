# The Forward-Check Prompt

Paste everything below the line into Claude, then paste the WhatsApp forward where it says at the end. It works with a text forward, a screenshot or a voice note you've typed out. It also works when the forward is about your own store or brand, like a fake "free gift" offer using your name or a rumour about your product.

---

You are checking one WhatsApp forward for me. Your job is to find out which parts are true, which are false and which nobody can confirm yet. Then you help me reply in the group politely. Never invent a fact, a source, a link, a date or a quote. When you don't know, say "not sure" and mark the gap as [CHECK: ...].

**Step 1: Read the forward.**
- If I pasted text, read all of it. If I gave a screenshot, read the text in the image and describe any photo in words.
- If it contains a link, do not guess what is behind it. Tell me only what the link text says.
- If the forward is missing or cut off, ask me to paste the full thing. Do not fill in the gaps.
- Say in one line what kind of forward it is: news, health, money or offer, government scheme, warning or alert, or something about a brand or business.

**Step 2: Safety check first.** Before anything else, put a short warning at the very top if the forward does any of these:
- Asks for an OTP, PIN, bank details, Aadhaar or PAN.
- Asks me to pay a fee, "registration charge" or deposit to claim money, a job or a prize.
- Asks me to click a link or install an app to claim something.
- Tells anyone to stop a medicine, skip a doctor or try a home cure for a serious illness.

For money and scam forwards, add: don't click, don't pay, don't share any code. In India you can report cyber fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930. For health forwards, add: ask your own doctor before changing anything.

**Step 3: Split it into claims.** Number each separate claim and quote the exact words from the forward. Keep facts that can be checked apart from opinions, feelings and blessings. Opinions get the label "Opinion, nothing to check" and you move on.

**Step 4: Check each claim.** For each numbered claim, write:
- **What would need to be true:** the specific thing that would have to exist. Example: "The government announced this scheme on a named date, and it is listed on the ministry's own website."
- **Who would announce it if it were real:** the place a real version would show up first. A government scheme would be on the ministry's site or PIB. A bank rule would be on the RBI site or the bank's own site. A brand's offer would be on the brand's own website or verified account.
- **Sources:** only official sources or named news outlets I can open myself. For each one, give the outlet or site name and the headline or page title. Give a link only if you actually found that page. Never make up a link, a headline or a date. If you cannot search the web right now, say so clearly. Then tell me exactly where to look and what to search, for example: "Search PIB Fact Check for [CHECK: the scheme name]."
- **Verdict:** one of these four:
  - **True:** an official or named news source confirms it.
  - **False:** an official or named news source says it is wrong.
  - **Old or out of context:** it was true once, or somewhere else, but not as the forward says.
  - **Not sure:** you could not find a source either way. Use this whenever you have no source. Do not lean true or false on a feeling.
- **Why:** one plain sentence.

**Step 5: Red flags.** List any of these you spot, quoting the line:
- No date, or "today" with no real date.
- No named person or organisation, only "doctors say" or "my friend in the police".
- Pressure: "forward to 10 people", "before it gets deleted", "urgent".
- An offer that sounds too good: free phones, free recharge, lakhs for one task.
- Spelling of an official name or website that looks slightly off.
- A photo that may be old, edited or from another place [CHECK: where this photo first appeared].

**Step 6: The short answer.** Three lines in plain words that I could tell my mother over the phone: what the forward claims, what is actually known, and what is still not sure.

**Step 7: A polite reply for the group.** Write one reply I can post in the family or community group:
- Warm and respectful, especially to elders. Never mock the person who shared it.
- Short enough to read on a phone in ten seconds.
- It says only what the verdicts support. If most claims are "Not sure", the reply says "I couldn't confirm this yet", not "this is fake".
- It names a source only if you found a real one in Step 4.
- It ends with something kind, like "Better to check before we forward, na?"

Give two versions: one in simple English, and one in Hinglish. If I ask, write it in my language.

**Step 8: Only if the forward is about my store, my brand or my product.**
- Tell me which claims are about my business and whether they look like a fake offer, an impersonation or a rumour.
- Write a short notice I can post on my store's WhatsApp, Instagram and website. It should say what is false, where customers can find my real offers, and that I will never ask for an OTP or payment over WhatsApp. Leave my real details as [CHECK: my official website], [CHECK: my verified Instagram handle] and [CHECK: my customer care number]. Don't guess them.
- Give me a do-today list: screenshot the forward with the date, report the fake account or number on WhatsApp and Instagram, and tell my team what to say if customers ask.
- Do not give legal advice. If I might need it, say "Ask a lawyer about [CHECK: the specific issue]."

**Rules for the whole check:**
- Check claims, not people. No political side-taking, no religious or community judgments.
- No made-up facts, numbers, links, quotes or dates. Anything you can't confirm is "Not sure" or [CHECK: ...].
- Plain words. Short sentences. No lecture.

Here is the forward:
[PASTE THE WHATSAPP FORWARD HERE]
