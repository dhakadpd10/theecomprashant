# Old Photo Restore Prompt

Paste everything below the line into ChatGPT or Gemini, attach the old photo, and fill in the last line. For best results, photograph the print flat, in daylight, without flash, and straight from above. If you plan to use the photo on your shop's "Our story" page or in a festival post, this prompt keeps it honest.

---

You are restoring one old family photo. Your job is to repair damage only. The result should look like the same photo on the day it was printed. It should not look like a new photo of the same people.

**Step 1: Look before you edit.** If no photo is attached, ask me for one and stop. If there is a photo, write down:
- how many people are in it, and where each one is (left, centre, right, front, back)
- every kind of damage you can see: scratches, dust, fold lines, tears, missing corners, stains, fading, blur, a studio stamp or handwriting
- any part where damage hides a detail, such as a face, a hand, jewellery or the edge of a saree

Do not say who the people are, where it was taken, the year or the occasion unless I told you. If I ask you to guess, mark every guess as [CHECK: ...].

**Step 2: The rules. They never bend.**
1. **Faces stay the same.** Keep each face's shape, eyes, nose, mouth, expression, age and skin tone. Keep moles, scars, wrinkles, spectacles and facial hair. Do not smooth skin, lighten skin, add makeup, open closed eyes, add a smile, show teeth or change where anyone is looking.
2. **Clothes stay the same.** Keep every pattern, border, fold and colour as it is: saree, dupatta, kurta, shirt, turban, cap or school uniform.
3. **Jewellery stays the same.** Keep every bangle, chain, mangalsutra, earring, nose ring, ring, bindi, sindoor and tilak exactly as shown. Do not add, remove, enlarge or polish any of it. If a piece is unclear, leave it unclear and list it.
4. **Add nothing new.** Add no people, objects, background, sky, furniture, text or borders. Remove no one, even if they are blurry or half cut off.
5. **Keep the frame.** Keep the pose, crop, angle and lighting direction. Do not straighten people or tidy the background.
6. **Fill damage with the plainest guess.** If a tear covers a small area, fill it only with a simple continuation of what is around it, such as plain wall, plain cloth or plain hair. List every fill.
7. **Do not rebuild a missing face.** If more than a small part of a face is gone, leave that damage visible and tell me. A made-up face of a real family member is worse than a scratch.
8. **Stop if you can't keep a face.** If your tool cannot repair the photo without changing a face, say so and stop. Do not hand me a changed face.

**Step 3: Restore.**
- Remove scratches, dust, spots, fold lines and stains.
- Fix fading and contrast so the photo looks like a good print, not a new digital photo.
- Sharpen gently, and only where the original detail is there to sharpen. Do not invent texture.
- Keep a little of the original film grain.
- **Colour:** follow my choice in the last line. If I chose "keep as it is", a black-and-white or sepia photo stays black-and-white or sepia. If I chose "add colour", every colour is a guess, and you must list each one as [CHECK: ...], for example [CHECK: saree colour guessed as maroon; the original is black and white].

**Step 4: Give me the photo, then this report.**
- **Repaired:** a short list of what you fixed and where.
- **Guessed:** every detail you had to fill in or guess, one per line, as [CHECK: what and where]. For example: [CHECK: bottom-left corner was torn; filled with plain floor; no feet or objects added]. If you guessed nothing, write "Nothing guessed."
- **Left alone:** any damage you chose not to fix and why, for example a missing face or a smudge over jewellery.
- **Not sure:** anything in the photo you could not make out. Don't describe it as if you could.

**Step 5: End your reply with this checklist, word for word:**

1. Keep the original print and the original scan. Never replace them with the restored copy.
2. Put the restored photo next to the original and check every face, one by one, with someone who knew them.
3. Label it "Restored photo" wherever you share it, including your shop page, ads and WhatsApp.

Plain words in the report. No praise for your own work.
