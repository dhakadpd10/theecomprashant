# Local Shop Poster Kit

Paste everything below the line into Claude, then attach a photo of the shop's current menu or board (or type what it says) where it says at the bottom. It works for any local shop, such as a tea stall, bakery, salon, kirana, tiffin service or tuition centre. Claude writes the Canva prompts. Paste those into Canva AI, or into Canva Craft if your account has it.

---

You are helping me sell poster and menu design to one local shop in India using free AI tools. I am a beginner. Your job is to:

1. Read the shop's current menu or board and say what to fix.
2. Write Canva prompts for 3 free samples: a menu, an offer post and a festival poster.
3. Write the WhatsApp message that sends the samples to the owner.
4. Help me set my own price.

Be practical and honest. Plain words, short sentences, no hype.

**Rules for the whole task**
- Never invent facts. That includes items, prices, offers, phone numbers, timings, addresses, festival dates, reviews and past clients.
- If you can't read something or don't know it, write [CHECK: what I need to confirm] and keep going.
- Never promise the owner more sales, footfall or results.
- Never suggest a price or market rate for my work. I set my own price in Step 5.
- Don't use other companies' logos or trademarks (soft drink brands, delivery apps, payment apps) unless the owner confirms they have them on their own board or have permission. Use plain text like "UPI accepted" instead of payment logos.

**Step 1: Get the basics.** Before anything else, ask me for anything below that I haven't given you. Ask all of it in one short list, then wait for my answers.
- Shop name and type (for example: chai stall, bakery, salon).
- City or area.
- Languages the customers read (for example: English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil).
- Who the customers are (for example: college students, office crowd, families).
- Which festival or occasion is coming up that the shop would care about. I will confirm the date. Do not assume it.
- Any offer the owner already runs. If none, the offer sample uses a clearly marked placeholder.
- Whether the shop has a logo, fixed colours or a signboard style.

**Step 2: Read the current menu or board.** If I attached a photo, read it carefully.
- First, write out everything you can see, exactly as written: items, prices, sections, phone number, timings and any other text.
- Mark anything blurry, cut off or unclear as [CHECK: ...].
- If there is no photo, ask me to type the text or describe the board. Don't guess.

Then list what to fix, most important first. Give a maximum of 8 points. Check for:
- **Readable from 2 metres:** text size, contrast, too many fonts, handwriting that's hard to read.
- **Prices:** missing, hard to find, crossed out, or inconsistent formatting (₹50 vs Rs.50 vs 50/-).
- **Structure:** items in no order, no sections, best sellers buried.
- **Missing info:** shop timings, phone or WhatsApp number, "UPI accepted", veg/non-veg marks for food, address or landmark.
- **Spelling and language:** typos, and mixed languages that confuse.
- **Clutter:** old stickers, faded print, too much text.
- **Phone view:** would this work as a WhatsApp status or Instagram post, or only on the wall?

For each point, write:
- **What I see:** quote the exact text or describe the spot.
- **Why it matters:** one sentence, from a customer's point of view.
- **The fix:** one sentence.

Be kind. This is someone's shop and their own work. The owner may read this list.

**Step 3: Write 3 Canva prompts.** Write one prompt for each sample. Each prompt must be ready to paste into Canva AI or Canva Craft and must include:
- The size.
- The style and mood (match the shop's colours and signboard style if known).
- The layout.
- The exact text to show.
- What to avoid.

Use only real items and prices from Step 2. Anything unconfirmed stays as [CHECK: ...] so I can fill it in.

1. **Menu**
   - Print version: A4 portrait, 210 × 297 mm.
   - Phone version: 1080 × 1350 px portrait for Instagram and WhatsApp.
   - Group items into clear sections. Put the 3 best sellers at the top. I will tell you which they are, or write [CHECK: best sellers].
   - Big prices, aligned the same way. Timings, phone number and "UPI accepted" at the bottom.

2. **Offer post**
   - Feed version: 1080 × 1350 px.
   - Status/story version: 1080 × 1920 px.
   - One offer only, in big text, with one line saying how to claim it.
   - If the owner has no confirmed offer, use "[CHECK: offer from owner]" as the headline text. Don't make one up.

3. **Festival poster**
   - Print version: A3 portrait, 297 × 420 mm (A4 also fine).
   - Status version: 1080 × 1920 px.
   - Festival greeting in the customers' language, the shop name and one line about timings or an offer.
   - Put the date as [CHECK: festival date] until I confirm it.
   - Keep religious images and symbols respectful and simple. Add a note telling me to ask the owner what they prefer.

After the 3 prompts, add this note for me in plain words:
- AI design tools often misspell words and prices inside images. After Canva makes the design, delete any AI-written text and type the real text and prices yourself in normal text boxes. Check every price against the photo.
- Add a small "SAMPLE" label in a corner of each design until the owner agrees to work with you.
- Some Canva AI features depend on your plan and region. If a feature isn't there, use a free template in the same size and apply the same text and layout.

**Step 4: The WhatsApp message.** Write a short first message that sends the 3 samples to the owner.
- Write it in English and also in [the language I chose in Step 1]. Keep each under 80 words.
- It must say:
  - Who I am, in one line (my name and that I live or study nearby). Use [MY NAME] and [MY AREA] as placeholders.
  - That I made 3 free sample designs for their shop. No charge and no pressure.
  - That they can see the samples in the attached images.
  - That if they like them, I can update them with their correct details and send final files for [MY PRICE] (I fill this in).
  - One simple question to reply to, for example: "Should I make the final version?"
- Do not invent experience, past clients or results, and do not claim the designs will increase sales.

Then write:
- **One follow-up**, sent only after 3 days of no reply, in 2 lines. If they still don't reply, I stop.
- **A 3-line script** for showing the samples on my phone if I visit the shop in person, at a quiet time, not during their rush hour.
- **A reminder for me:** only message a number the shop shows publicly (on its board, Google listing or WhatsApp Business). If the owner says no, thank them and stop.

**Step 5: My price sheet.** Help me set my own price. Don't suggest one. Ask me to fill in this table, then do the maths.

| Item | My number |
|---|---|
| Hours to make one poster (including edits) | [FILL] |
| What I want to earn per hour (₹) | [FILL] |
| Canva cost per month (₹0 if free plan) | [FILL] |
| Posters I expect to make per month | [FILL] |
| Printing cost per poster, if I handle printing (₹) | [FILL] |
| Travel cost per visit (₹) | [FILL] |
| Free edits I include | [FILL] |

Calculate:
- **My lowest price per poster** = (hours × hourly amount) + (Canva cost ÷ posters per month) + printing + travel. Round up to a clean number.
- **Three simple packages**, using my lowest price as the base. Leave the final ₹ for me to decide:
  - Single poster.
  - Menu plus 2 offer posts.
  - Monthly pack (for example: 4 posts plus 1 festival poster).
- **Extras** to charge separately: urgent same-day work, extra edits beyond the free ones, and printing.

Then add:
- "Before you fix your price, ask 2 or 3 local printers or designers what they charge for a menu or poster: [CHECK: local rates]."
- "Pay varies by city, shop and how good your samples look. Your first yes may take many messages."

**Step 6: Final check.** End with a short checklist I tick before sending anything to the owner:
- Every price matches the photo or the owner's word.
- No [CHECK: ...] is left in the final designs.
- Spelling is checked in every language.
- Sizes are correct (print vs phone).
- The "SAMPLE" label is on.
- No logos or trademarks I don't have permission for.
