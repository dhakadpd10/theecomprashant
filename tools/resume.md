# One-Page CV and Cover Note Prompt

Copy everything below the line into free ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. Then fill in the three boxes at the bottom: your real details, 3 messages you wrote yourself, and the job listing. It works for students, 9-to-5ers, freelancers and people who run their own shop or online store. Leave out your Aadhaar, PAN, bank details and full home address. A CV never needs them.

---

You are helping me write a one-page CV and a 3-line cover note for one specific job. Use only the facts I give you. Never invent a job, degree, skill, tool, number, date, company, award or result. If something would make the CV stronger but I haven't told you, don't fill it in. Write [CHECK: what you need from me] in that spot and I will add it or delete it.

**Step 1: Check what I gave you.** Read my three boxes below. If the job listing or my details are missing, ask me for them and stop there. If my 3 messages are missing, go ahead with plain, simple English and tell me you did.

**Step 2: Learn my style, not my facts.** Read my 3 messages only to understand how I write: sentence length, how formal I am, the words I use and the words I never use. Don't copy anything personal from them. If a message mentions a fact about my work, don't use it unless that same fact is also in my details. Write the CV and cover note so they sound like me on a good day: clear, polite and professional. Avoid words I would never say, such as "synergy", "dynamic", "passionate go-getter" and "results-driven".

**Step 3: Read the job listing.** List the 5 to 8 things this employer cares about most, in their own words where you can quote them. Then make a table with three columns:
- **They want:** the requirement.
- **I have:** the exact fact from my details that matches it, or "Nothing yet".
- **Honest gap?** Yes or No.

Never turn a gap into a claim. If I don't have something, the CV says nothing about it. You can suggest one honest line I could add if it is true, marked [CHECK: is this true?].

**Step 4: Write the CV. One page, about 350 to 500 words.** Use plain text with simple headings so I can paste it into Google Docs or Word. Use this order, and skip any section I gave no facts for:
1. Name, city, phone, email, and a LinkedIn or portfolio link if I gave one.
2. A 2-line summary aimed at this job, using only my real facts.
3. Experience, newest first. This includes jobs, internships, freelance work, family business or running my own store. For each one, give 2 to 4 bullet points that start with an action word. Where I gave a real number (orders, customers, ₹ sales, hours saved, team size), use it exactly. Where a number would help but I gave none, write [CHECK: how many / how much?]. Never guess a number or round it up.
4. Skills and tools. Include only the ones I listed, and put the ones this job asks for first.
5. Education, with marks or CGPA only if I gave them.
6. Optional: certificates, languages or projects, only from my details.

Rules for the CV:
- Turn a duty into a result only when my facts support it. "Handled customer messages" stays as it is unless I said what changed because of it.
- Don't reword my job titles to sound bigger.
- If I run or ran my own business, describe it as exactly what it was (for example "Ran my own online store selling X"). Don't call me a CEO or founder unless I used that word.
- Use Indian English and Indian formats: ₹ for money, DD Mon YYYY dates, and CTC or notice period only if I gave them.

**Step 5: Write the cover note. Exactly 3 lines, in my style.**
- Line 1: which role I'm applying for and the one fact that makes me a fit.
- Line 2: one more real fact that matches something specific in the listing.
- Line 3: a polite, simple close (for example, that I'm happy to share more or talk this week).

It should work as an email body, a LinkedIn message or a note on Naukri, Internshala or a WhatsApp application. No flattery about the company that I didn't give you a reason for.

**Step 6: The check list.** After the CV and cover note, list:
- Every [CHECK: ...] in the order it appears, so I can fix them in one go.
- Any gap from Step 3 the employer will probably notice, with one honest way to handle it (learn it, show a small project, or just leave it).
- Anything in my details that looked inconsistent, like overlapping dates or two different numbers for the same thing. Ask me which is right instead of choosing one.

Keep everything short and plain. No hype. If you aren't sure about something, ask me rather than guess.
