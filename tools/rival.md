# The Rival Ad Teardown

Open the Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library), set the country to India and the type to "All ads", and search for a rival brand. Pick 5 ads from rivals who sell to your buyer, and fill in one capture block per ad at the bottom of the prompt. Copy the text by hand and describe the creative in your own words. No scraping tools needed. Then paste everything below the line into Claude, with your filled blocks and your brand details where it says.

---

You are running a Rival Ad Teardown. I will give you 5 competitor ads that I copied from the Meta Ad Library. Your job is to find three things. First, the claim every rival keeps repeating. Second, the buyer doubt that none of them answer. Third, three hooks I could use to own that missing angle, but only if it is true for my brand.

**Ground rules. Follow these the whole way through.**
- Work only from what I paste. Do not open links, browse or guess what an ad shows. If something is missing, write [CHECK: what's missing] and carry on.
- The Ad Library shows what rivals are running. It does not show their sales, spend, clicks or results for normal ads. Never say an ad "works", "converts" or "is their best seller". An ad that has run for a long time may be working, or someone may have forgotten to switch it off. Call it "long-running" and nothing more.
- Never invent facts about my brand or theirs. That includes prices, reviews, ratings, certifications, delivery times, guarantees and customer numbers. Mark anything you'd need to know as [CHECK: ...].
- Do not copy a rival's wording, slogans or creative ideas into my hooks. We want the gap they leave, not their ad.
- Do not write anything that names a rival, mocks them or says they are fake, unsafe or lying. Comparison claims are only allowed if I can prove them, and they must be fair under ASCI rules in India.
- No health, medical, "guaranteed results" or before/after promises unless I give you proof. Flag these with [CHECK: proof needed].

**Step 1: Check the input.**
List the 5 ads in a short table with these columns: Brand, Format, Started running, Still active, Versions. If I gave you fewer than 5 ads, or a block is mostly empty, tell me which one. Ask whether I want to add more before you go on. 3 ads is the minimum. With fewer than 5, warn me that the patterns will be weaker.

**Step 2: Take each ad apart.**
For each ad, write:
- **Hook:** the first line or the first 3 seconds, quoted exactly from what I pasted.
- **Main claim:** the one promise the ad makes, in 10 words or fewer.
- **Proof shown:** reviews, numbers, demo, founder, UGC, certificate, or "none".
- **Offer:** discount, free shipping, COD, combo, gift, or "none shown".
- **Buyer it's aimed at:** who this ad seems to speak to.
- **What it leaves out:** price, delivery time, returns, size or fit, ingredients or material, who it's not for.

**Step 3: The jury. Six buyers, each judging alone.**
Read all 5 ads as each of these Indian online shoppers, one at a time. Each juror answers on their own and never sees the others' answers.

1. **COD doubter:** has never heard of these brands and only pays cash on delivery. "Will it even arrive? Is it fake? Can I pay at the door?"
2. **Marketplace comparer:** checks Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho before buying from any Instagram ad. "Why should I buy here and not there, and is it cheaper there?"
3. **Burned-before buyer:** once ordered from an Instagram ad and got something that looked nothing like the video. "Show me the real thing, not the shoot."
4. **Spec reader:** wants the facts. Material, size, ingredients, weight, how long it lasts, what's in the box.
5. **3-second scroller:** on a phone, thumb moving. "Do I understand what this is and why I'd want it before I scroll past?"
6. **Return worrier:** buying for themselves or as a gift, maybe for a festival. "What if it doesn't fit or they don't like it? Will it come before Diwali? Who do I call?"

For each juror, write:
- **Most convincing ad:** which one, and the exact line that did it.
- **Still doubting:** the question they still have after seeing all 5 ads, in their own voice, in one sentence.
- **Answered by any ad?** Yes (say which) or No.

**Step 4: The verdict.**
- **The crowded claim:** the claim or angle that most rivals repeat. Say how many of the 5 ads use it, and quote each one briefly. If every ad sounds the same, my ad should not say this too.
- **The unanswered doubt:** the doubt that the most jurors raised and that no ad answered. Say how many jurors raised it. If two doubts tie, list both.
- **Other gaps:** up to 3 more doubts or angles nobody covers, ranked by how many jurors raised them.
- **Formats in use:** one line on what the rivals lean on (for example, all static images, all founder videos, all discount-led). Say whether a different format could stand out. Remember that this is a guess, not proof.

**Step 5: Can I own the gap?**
A missing angle only helps me if it's true for my brand. Use my brand details below. For the unanswered doubt, tell me:
- **What I'd need to say:** the plain answer to the doubt.
- **Proof I'd need to show:** the specific thing that makes the answer believable, such as a real unboxing, a written return policy, a delivery-time screenshot, a real review, or a size chart.
- **Do I have it?** Yes, No, or [CHECK: ...], based only on what I told you.

If I don't have the proof yet, say so plainly. Then give me the smallest thing I could do this week to get it. Don't write hooks that promise something I can't back up.

**Step 6: Three hooks for the missing angle.**
Write 3 different hooks that answer the unanswered doubt. Make them different in approach, for example one question, one demo, one plain statement. For each hook, give:
- **Spoken line (first 3 seconds):** under 15 words, plain Hinglish or English to match my brand voice.
- **On-screen text:** 6 words or fewer.
- **What the viewer sees:** the first shot, described simply.
- **Juror it's for:** which of the 6.
- **Proof it needs:** what must appear in the ad to make it true. Mark anything you don't know as [CHECK: ...].
- **Risk:** one honest line on why it might not work.

**Step 7: How to test it.**
Give me a simple test plan. Run the 3 hooks as separate ads to the same audience, with the same offer and the same budget each, and judge them on my own numbers (cost per purchase, hold rate at 3 seconds, click-through rate). Label any budget or duration you suggest as an example, not a rule. Remind me that rivals' ads tell me what they're saying, while my own results tell me what works for me.

Finish with a short list called **Do today**: at most 5 items, most important first.

Plain words. Short sentences. No hype.
