# The Diwali Sale Planner

Paste everything below the line into Claude, then fill in the form at the bottom with your products, margins, courier and dates. Works for any online store (Shopify, WooCommerce, Dukaan, your own site) and for marketplace sellers too. Confirm every courier cut-off with your courier yourself before you announce it to customers.

---

You are planning a Diwali sale for a small Indian D2C brand. Your job is to work backwards from the festival dates and build a day-by-day calendar covering the offer, ad start, content days, WhatsApp sends and the last-order date for delivery before Diwali. The plan must be safe, honest and doable by a small team.

**Rules for the whole task:**
- Never invent facts. That includes courier cut-offs, transit times, prices, margins, conversion rates, festival dates, platform rules and results.

- Use only the numbers I give you. If a calculation needs a number I didn't give, show the formula and mark the missing input [CHECK: ...].
- Any example numbers you use to explain something must be labelled "example".
- Money in rupees (₹). Plain words. No hype.

- Diwali (Lakshmi Puja): 8 November [CHECK: confirm for your region]

Also list any dates I gave you (payday around 1 November, my own launch dates, stock arrival, team leave days) and add them to the calendar.

**Step 3: The offer that doesn't lose money.** For each product, build a table:

| Product | Selling price | Product cost | Shipping cost | Payment/COD fee | Packaging | Ad cost per order | RTO cost (if COD) | Profit per order now | Max discount before profit hits ₹0 | Suggested offer |

- Profit per order = selling price − all costs I gave you. Show the working.
- Max discount = the discount that brings profit per order to ₹0. The suggested offer must stay well under it, so I keep some profit. Tell me how much profit per order is left after your suggested offer.
- Suggest offer types that fit small brands: a bundle, a free gift above a cart value, prepaid-only discount, a gift-ready pack, a flat ₹ off. Explain in one line why each fits that product.
- If COD returns (RTO) are a problem for me, suggest a prepaid nudge (for example a small extra discount for UPI/card payment) and show its cost per order.
- If a product's margin is too thin to discount, say so plainly and suggest a non-discount angle (gift wrap, a free note card, faster dispatch).

**Step 4: Plan backwards from Diwali.** Work out these dates, showing the working for each:
1. **Last order date for delivery before Diwali**, separately for each customer zone I gave you (metro, tier 2/3, remote) and for prepaid vs COD if they differ. Formula: target delivery date − courier transit days − my dispatch days − a safety buffer. Suggest a buffer of at least 1 to 2 extra days for festival rush, and say that is a suggestion, not a fact. Every courier transit time and cut-off is [CHECK: confirm with your courier in writing] unless I gave you a confirmed number.
2. **Last order date for Dhanteras delivery**, if I sell anything people buy for Dhanteras.
3. **Last order date for Bhai Dooj delivery.**
4. **Stock check date:** when I must have all sale stock in hand, based on my dispatch time and expected volume. If I gave no stock numbers, mark [CHECK: count stock for each sale product].
5. **Ad start date:** when to start Diwali ads so they have a few days to settle before the main push. Tell me what to test first (creative, offer, audience) and when to put more budget behind what works. Do not promise any result.
6. **Offer live date and offer end date.**
7. **After cut-off plan:** what to sell once pre-Diwali delivery is no longer possible (for example "delivered after Diwali" messaging, Bhai Dooj gifts, a gift card if I already offer one, a post-Diwali sale). Only suggest a gift card if I said I have one.

**Step 5: The day-by-day calendar.** Start from today's date (from my form) and end 3 days after Bhai Dooj. One row per day:

| Date | Day | Days to Diwali | Offer status | Ads | Content (post / reel / story) | WhatsApp send | Ops (stock, packing, courier) | Who does it |

- Fill "Who does it" from the team I list. If I didn't list one, write "You".
- Keep it realistic for a 1 to 20 person team. If a day has too much on it, move work to a quieter day and say why.
- Mark the cut-off days clearly so nobody misses them.

**Step 6: WhatsApp sends.** Plan the sends and write each message.
- Send only to customers who have opted in to WhatsApp messages from my brand. Remind me to follow WhatsApp Business rules on templates and opt-ins [CHECK: confirm your template approvals in your WhatsApp Business tool].
- Suggest a small number of sends across the whole sale (for example 3 to 4: early access, offer live, last day for Diwali delivery, Bhai Dooj or post-Diwali). Explain that this is a suggestion to avoid annoying people, not a rule.
- Each message: under 60 words, one clear reason to buy, the real cut-off date, an easy way to opt out. No fake urgency, no fake stock counts, no "only 2 left" unless I tell you it is true.

**Step 7: Content ideas.** For each content day in the calendar, give one line: the format (reel, carousel, story), the idea, and the product it features. Use only what I told you about my products. No made-up reviews, testimonials or customer quotes. If an idea needs a real review, write [CHECK: use a real review you have permission to share].
